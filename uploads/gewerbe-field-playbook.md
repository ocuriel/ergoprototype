# Commercial Lines Field Playbook — Germany
## Industries, coverages, need-spotting, and objection handling for the SMB market

A working tool for the commercial SMB conversation: the sixteen industry groupings that make up most of the German small-commercial market, the covers that matter in each, the cues that tell you to raise them, and the objections you will actually hear — with the move for each. Use it before, during, or after a client meeting.

**How the tiers work**

- **Must-have** — bind before the doors open. Includes covers that are legally or contractually mandatory (motor liability per vehicle, professional indemnity where professional law requires it) and the covers no serious advisor lets a client operate without.
- **Recommended** — raise on every qualifying account. The natural second and third policies in the bundle.
- **Situational** — know the cue, bring it up when it appears.

**Standing rules on every account, regardless of industry**

1. Underinsurance (Unterversicherung) is a standard check item on every document. Confirm sums insured against current values, and confirm whether a waiver of underinsurance (Unterversicherungsverzicht) and a premium-free buffer (Vorsorge) apply in the current terms — and at what level.
2. Never quote a specific sum insured, sublimit, deductible, or premium from memory. Name the feature, then confirm the figure in the current policy conditions (AVB) or tariff documentation. Coverage scope changes by tariff generation.
3. § 19 VVG (pre-contractual disclosure) and § 6/§ 61 VVG (advice and documentation duty) are not compliance overhead — the documented advice record (Beratungsprotokoll) is a trust artifact in this market. Use it visibly.
4. Statutory accident insurance through the Berufsgenossenschaft covers employees' workplace and commuting accidents and occupational diseases automatically. It is never something to sell — but its boundaries (leisure time, benefit level, the owner personally) are where the supplementary conversation legitimately starts.

# Part 1 — The 16 industry groupings

## 1. Construction & Finishing Trades (Bau- und Ausbauhandwerk)
**Typical businesses:** Electrical, plumbing/heating/climate (SHK), carpentry & joinery, tile & screed, painting & drywall, roofing & scaffolding
**Opening question:** "Who is on the hook if your work causes a loss months after handover?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht with a construction wording** — bodily injury and property damage from the work, including the two clauses that decide claims in the trades: damage to the object being worked on (Bearbeitungs-/Tätigkeitsschäden) and losses surfacing after acceptance. *Signal:* every firm working on other people's property — i.e. all of them. *Claim picture:* a solder joint fails weeks after handover and floods a finished basement, a mid-five-figure loss. Verify the scope of the working-damage clause in the current conditions.
- **Motor/fleet cover** — motor liability is legally mandatory per vehicle. From several vehicles up, review a fleet solution; the loaded van is the firm's single largest loss scenario.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover (Geschäftsinhalt + Betriebskosten/Betriebsunterbrechung)** — workshop, warehouse, machinery at replacement value, with buffer and waiver of underinsurance. The fixed-cost cover carries rent, payroll, and leases when nothing runs after a fire or burglary.

**Recommended**
- **Bauleistungsversicherung** — protects the unfinished structure and delivered-but-uninstalled materials. *Signal:* new builds, shell construction, major renovations. The owner's finished-building policy does not respond here.
- **Montageversicherung** — for electrical, HVAC, and plant installation during the installation phase, including photovoltaic installs.
- **Kautionsversicherung** — warranty and performance bonds instead of withheld retentions. *Business impact:* frees the bank credit line — a liquidity argument, not an insurance argument.
- **Tools in transit** — off-premises extension in the contents policy or a transport cover for tools in the van and on site. *Signal:* "my whole setup lives in the van." Verify scope and overnight/vehicle clauses in the conditions.

**Situational**
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht** — the moment the firm designs rather than only executes (planning services, design-and-build).
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — disputes over payment and warranty claims are daily business in construction, not the exception.
- **Gruppen-Unfall, bKV, bAV** — the statutory accident cover ends at the workplace and pays no pain-and-suffering-level benefits; the owner is often not covered at all. In the skilled-labor shortage, the number-one retention argument.
- **Cyberversicherung** — quoting and invoicing are digital; fake-invoice fraud against the client increasingly hits trade firms.

## 2. Cleaning & Facility Services
**Typical businesses:** Contract cleaning, office & practice cleaning, glass & facade cleaning, specialty cleaning, caretaker/janitorial services, post-construction cleaning
**Opening question:** "You work with master keys inside other people's buildings — what happens when a key goes missing or something breaks under your cloth?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — the classic loss is the slip on a freshly mopped floor. The two clauses that matter sit exactly where cleaners operate: **damage to the property being worked on** and **key loss (Schlüsselverlust)** — verify scope and sublimits in the conditions. *Claim picture:* a master key to an office complex is lost and the entire locking system has to be replaced.
- **Motor/fleet cover** — crews drive multiple sites daily.

**Recommended**
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — a headcount-heavy industry; employment disputes (dismissal, wage claims) are the most frequent legal case.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover** — machine park and warehouse, routinely underestimated in growing firms.
- **Gruppen-Unfallversicherung** — supplements the statutory accident cover, especially for glass and facade work at height.

**Situational**
- **Cyberversicherung** — access codes, building plans, and client data in the scheduling software.
- **bKV** — retention in a high-turnover sector; small budget, outsized effect.

## 3. Landscaping & Grounds (GaLaBau, incl. winter service)
**Typical businesses:** Landscape construction, grounds maintenance, tree care, paving, winter/gritting service, irrigation
**Opening question:** "Who carries the gritting-duty liability when someone falls on a lot you maintain?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — including assumed **traffic-safety and gritting duties** (winter service explicitly covered — verify in the conditions) and the environmental modules for pesticide and fuel use. *Claim picture:* black-ice fall on a maintained parking lot, bodily injury with a long recovery.
- **Mobile machinery cover (fahrbare Maschinen)** — excavators, loaders, ride-on mowers: high values, openly accessible, prime theft targets.
- **Fleet** — tow vehicles with trailer operations.

**Recommended**
- **Bauleistungsversicherung** — on new installations and larger outdoor projects.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover** — hall, workshop, seasonal equipment.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — warranty disputes over plantings and surfaces.

**Situational**
- **Kautionsversicherung** — on public tenders and large projects.
- **Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — physically hard work; the statutory cover ends at the gate.
- **Cyberversicherung** — once quoting and billing run digitally.

## 4. Food Service & Hospitality
**Typical businesses:** Restaurants, cafés, fast food & franchise units, bars, hotels & guesthouses, catering
**Opening question:** "If the health authority closes your business tomorrow — how long can you carry the fixed costs?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — guest injuries, food liability, cloakroom, alcohol service; the typical high-street operation fits the compact SME tariff lines.
- **Geschäftsinhaltsversicherung** — kitchen, refrigeration, interior at replacement value; buffer and waiver of underinsurance are real money here, because hospitality reinvests constantly.
- **Fixed-cost / business-interruption cover** — after the kitchen fire, rent, payroll, and leases keep running.
- **Betriebsschließungsversicherung** — the hospitality special case: ordered closure under the Infection Protection Act (salmonella, norovirus). Since the pandemic, every restaurateur knows the scenario — the only question is whether they hold the cover, and on which terms.

**Recommended**
- **Commercial building cover (gewerbliche Gebäudeversicherung)** — where the property is owned.
- **Cyberversicherung** — reservation system, POS, guest data.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — employment law and disputes around the commercial lease dominate.

**Situational**
- **bKV** — in the hospitality labor shortage, one of the few benefits that genuinely differentiates an employer.
- **Gruppen-Unfall** — kitchen and service, cut and burn risks beyond the statutory baseline.
- **Kunstversicherung** — hotels and upscale venues with art on the walls.

## 5. Retail
**Typical businesses:** Fashion & shoes, food & delicatessen, electronics & telecom, furniture & interiors, sports & leisure, kiosk & convenience
**Opening question:** "When did you last check your sum insured against the actual stock value on the floor — including the seasonal peak?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — customer traffic on the floor: slips, falling goods, traffic-safety duties. The classic case for the compact SME tariff lines.
- **Geschäftsinhaltsversicherung** — stock is the working capital. Replacement-value settlement, a premium-free buffer, and the waiver of underinsurance are the three features that decide the sale — confirm each in the current conditions.
- **Fixed-cost / business-interruption cover** — prime-location rent keeps running after the water damage.

**Recommended**
- **Cyberversicherung** — the moment a webshop or digital customer data exists; POS downtime is revenue downtime.
- **Elektronikversicherung** — tills, scanners, digital signage.
- **Transport cover (goods)** — own delivery traffic and stock movement between branches.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — commercial tenancy law and employment law.

**Situational**
- **Betriebsschließungsversicherung** — in food retail.
- **Commercial building cover** — where owned.
- **Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — retention in branch operations.

## 6. Real Estate & Property Management
**Typical businesses:** Property managers (WEG & rental), portfolio landlords, estate agents, small-scale development, facility owners, holiday letting
**Opening question:** "You manage other people's money — is the liability for that insured the way professional law requires?"

**Must-have**
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflichtversicherung** — mandatory by professional law for residential property managers and estate agents; verify the current statutory minimum requirements per license. Errors in accounting, deadlines, or resolution execution are pure financial losses — the Betriebshaftpflicht does not respond.
- **Betriebshaftpflicht incl. property-owner liability (Haus- und Grundbesitzerhaftpflicht)** — traffic-safety duties on managed and owned buildings.
- **Commercial building cover** — for portfolio holders the core product: fire, water, storm — and actively put the natural-hazards (Elementar) question on the table.

**Recommended**
- **Mietkautionsversicherung** — the natural hook in the landlord conversation.
- **Photovoltaik cover** — PV on existing roofs is growing fast; the installation is not automatically cleanly captured in the building policy — verify the allocation.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — tenancy and condo-association disputes are daily business.
- **Bauleistungsversicherung** — on refurbishment and roof projects in the portfolio.

**Situational**
- **Cyberversicherung** — managers hold the bank details and master data of entire owner associations.
- **Kunstversicherung** — representative buildings and lobbies.

## 7. Professional & Financial Services
**Typical businesses:** Management consulting, financial & insurance intermediaries, payroll & bookkeeping services, surveyors & experts, recruiting, translation
**Opening question:** "Your product is your advice — what happens when a piece of advice was demonstrably wrong and the client puts a number on the damage?"

**Must-have**
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflichtversicherung** — the core product of the segment. Legally or professionally mandatory for several licensed intermediary and advisory professions — verify mandatory status and minimum requirements per profession. *Claim picture:* a flawed deadline or needs recommendation, the client quantifies a financial loss; without PI the advisor pays privately.
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — the office and visitor risk, small but necessary.
- **Cyberversicherung** — client data is the most sensitive asset; a data leak is simultaneously a GDPR case and a reputation loss.

**Recommended**
- **Contents + Elektronikversicherung** — office tech and mobile devices.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — fee disputes and employment law.
- **Business travel cover (Dienstreiseversicherung)** — advisory firms travel.

**Situational**
- **bAV / bKV** — standard benefits in the fight for qualified staff.
- **Gruppen-Unfallversicherung** — closes the gap between statutory cover and real income protection, including for the owner.

## 8. IT & Digital Services
**Typical businesses:** Software development, IT system houses & managed services, web development, IT consulting, hosting & cloud, e-commerce services
**Opening question:** "If your update stops the client's production line — who carries the downtime?"

**Must-have**
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht for IT service providers** — errors in code, configuration, or advice almost never cause property damage; they cause pure financial loss at the client. Verify the IT wording and its combination with the Betriebshaftpflicht.
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — the classic operational risk alongside, usually packaged.
- **Cyberversicherung** — doubly critical: the firm's own downtime *and* the credibility question toward clients whose systems it manages. An IT provider without its own cyber cover loses the argument at its own customer.

**Recommended**
- **Elektronikversicherung** — development and server hardware.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — disputes over project acceptance and work contracts.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover** — once own premises and equipment exist.

**Situational**
- **bAV** — near hygiene-factor status in IT recruiting.
- **Business travel cover** — project work on the client's site.

## 9. Agencies, Marketing & Media
**Typical businesses:** Advertising & creative agencies, social media & performance marketing, photo & video production, PR & communications, design & branding, publishing & content
**Opening question:** "Who is liable when a campaign infringes an image, trademark, or personality right — you or your client?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht + Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht** — the media risk (copyright, trademark, and personality-right infringement; flawed campaign execution with a quantified client loss) is a financial-loss risk. Verify which module carries these scenarios in the current conditions before quoting.
- **Cyberversicherung** — client access to ad accounts and shops, management of third-party media budgets.

**Recommended**
- **Elektronikversicherung** — camera, edit, and production gear, especially on location.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover** — studio and fit-out.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — fee and usage-rights disputes.

**Situational**
- **Business travel cover** — productions and pitches off-site.
- **Kunstversicherung** — agencies with collections or exhibition space.

## 10. Healthcare & Care Services
**Typical businesses:** Physiotherapy, occupational & speech therapy, alternative practitioners (Heilpraktiker), outpatient care services, midwife practices, podiatry
**Opening question:** "A treatment-error allegation eventually hits every practice — who runs your defense and pays a justified claim?"

**Must-have**
- **Business & professional liability (Betriebs-/Berufshaftpflicht)** — bodily injury from treatment is the core risk; verify professional-law insurance requirements per profession. The unjustified allegation costs money too: liability cover doubles as passive legal defense.
- **Contents + Elektronikversicherung** — practice fit-out and treatment devices, often financed and worth more than assumed.
- **Fixed-cost cover** — if the practice stands still after water damage, rent and salaries keep running while nothing can be billed.

**Recommended**
- **Cyberversicherung** — patient data is health data, the most sensitive GDPR category; practice-software downtime paralyzes billing.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — billing, employment, and professional-law conflicts; sector-specific healthcare variants exist — check availability.
- **Fleet** — for outpatient care services the vehicle fleet *is* the business.

**Situational**
- **Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — care and therapy are physically demanding, and a company health benefit in a health profession is a credible retention tool.
- **Betriebsschließungsversicherung** — check for facilities within the scope of the Infection Protection Act.

## 11. Personal Care & Fitness
**Typical businesses:** Hair salons, cosmetics & nail studios, barbershops, gyms, yoga & personal training, non-therapeutic massage
**Opening question:** "What happens when a treatment or a training session causes a health injury to the client?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — treatment injuries (chemical burn from coloring, allergic reaction), floor slips, training injuries from instruction or equipment errors. Classic compact-tariff high-street segment.
- **Contents + fixed-cost cover** — fit-out and equipment, plus the rent that keeps running after the loss.

**Recommended**
- **Cyberversicherung** — online booking, member and billing data.
- **Elektronikversicherung** — studio and treatment technology.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — tenancy and employment law.

**Situational**
- **bKV / Gruppen-Unfall** — retention in a high-churn industry.
- **Commercial building cover** — where owned.

## 12. Automotive Trades
**Typical businesses:** Independent workshops, body & paint, tire service, detailing, car dealers, towing & roadside
**Opening question:** "Customer vehicles sit on your lot every night — whose insurance pays when something happens there?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht with an automotive-trade wording** — the critical point is the customer vehicle in custody: damage while moving, working on, or storing third-party vehicles is classically excluded from a standard liability policy. Verify the trade-and-dealer motor solution (Handel und Handwerk) including dealer plates and test drives before promising anything.
- **Geschäftsinhaltsversicherung** — lifts, diagnostics, parts inventory.
- **Environmental modules** — oils, fuels, batteries: verify the environmental liability/damage scope in the conditions.

**Recommended**
- **Fixed-cost cover** — the workshop stops, the costs don't.
- **Maschinenversicherung** — for high-value stationary equipment.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — warranty disputes are a permanent condition in the auto trade.

**Situational**
- **Cyberversicherung** — workshop planning, diagnostics, and customer data.
- **Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — workshop trades in the skilled-labor shortage.
- **Kautionsversicherung** — where franchise or partner contracts demand bonds.

## 13. Education & Training
**Typical businesses:** Private training providers, tutoring institutes, driving schools, music & art schools, coaching & seminars, language schools
**Opening question:** "You carry a duty of supervision over other people's — often underage — children. Is it insured the way you're liable?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht** — breach of supervision duty and participant accidents on premises and at events. With minors, parents' liability expectations are high and emotional.
- **Contents + Elektronikversicherung** — classrooms, tech, and for music schools the instruments (verify valuation and off-premises cover).

**Recommended**
- **Cyberversicherung** — participant and payment data, online course platforms.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — contract and fee disputes with participants and instructors.
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht** — once certified qualifications, examinations, or advisory services are promised.

**Situational**
- **Fleet** — driving schools are primarily a motor risk; verify the dedicated driving-school vehicle wording separately.
- **Gruppen-Unfallversicherung** — participant accident cover as a quality marker for private providers; explain the boundary to statutory pupil accident insurance cleanly.
- **Business travel cover** — traveling instructors and trainers.

## 14. Events & Entertainment
**Typical businesses:** Event agencies, event technology & rental, trade-fair construction, catering & mobile food, DJs & artist services, wedding & event planning
**Opening question:** "Your most expensive gear is least protected exactly when it earns money — in transit and on the floor. True for you too?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht with an organizer wording (Veranstalterhaftpflicht)** — visitor injuries, structures, assumed traffic-safety duties; clarify in advance which event sizes and types the tariff carries.
- **Elektronikversicherung + transport cover** — sound, light, video: mobile, rented, or loaned out, overnight in the van. The stationary contents policy ends at the warehouse door.
- **Fleet** — vans and trailers are the backbone.

**Recommended**
- **Montageversicherung** — stage and booth build-up and tear-down; verify the boundary to liability cover.
- **Contents** — warehouse and workshop.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — disputes over cancellations, contracts, fees.

**Situational**
- **Cyberversicherung** — ticketing and participant data.
- **Gruppen-Unfall** — riggers and build crews beyond the statutory baseline.
- **Event cancellation (Veranstaltungsausfall)** — regularly requested; a specialty line with narrow appetite. Log the need and clarify availability and terms case by case rather than improvising an answer.

## 15. Light Manufacturing & Wholesale Distribution
**Typical businesses:** Metal & plastics processing, small food production, packaging & assembly, technical wholesale, building-materials trade, import & distribution
**Opening question:** "If your key machine stops tomorrow or a product has to be recalled — which of the two ruins you faster?"

**Must-have**
- **Betriebshaftpflicht / industrial liability with extended product liability** — the moment goods are produced, processed, or sold under the firm's own name, the extended product liability wording (erweiterte Produkthaftpflicht) is the core; beyond a certain size and risk depth this becomes an industrial-lines placement rather than a standard commercial tariff. Raise **recall costs (Rückrufkosten)** proactively — a recall hits the faultless supplier in the chain too.
- **Contents + Maschinenversicherung** — production plant and warehouses; machinery breakdown is its own risk next to fire.
- **Business interruption** — the bottleneck is existential here: one machine, one warehouse, one customer.

**Recommended**
- **Transport cover (goods / carrier liability)** — inbound and outbound goods, own delivery traffic; in wholesale the second core product.
- **Fleet** — delivery operations.
- **Cyberversicherung** — production IT and merchandise management; ransomware downtime is the modern business interruption.

**Situational**
- **Montageversicherung** — where delivery includes installation.
- **Photovoltaik cover** — factory roofs.
- **Kautionsversicherung** — supply and framework contracts with bond requirements.
- **bAV / Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — retaining skilled industrial workers.
- **Betriebsschließungsversicherung** — in food production.

## 16. Agriculture & Direct Marketing
**Typical businesses:** Arable farming, livestock, special crops & orchards, agricultural contractors, farm shops & direct marketing, energy farming (biogas/PV)
**Opening question:** "Your operation is home, machine park, warehouse, and company in one — where does private end and commercial begin for you?"

**Must-have**
- **Agricultural liability (landwirtschaftliche Betriebshaftpflicht)** — animal risk, emissions, traffic-safety duties, harvest work on and beside public roads; verify the wording and underwriting appetite for the specific farm type in advance.
- **Agricultural contents cover (landwirtschaftliche Inhaltsversicherung)** — harvest, supplies, equipment, and livestock within the scope of the conditions.
- **Mobile machinery + commercial building cover** — tractors and harvest technology tie up enormous capital; farm buildings against fire, storm, natural hazards.

**Recommended**
- **Photovoltaik cover** — farm roofs and ground-mounted; for many farms now a revenue line of its own.
- **Firmen-Rechtsschutz** — lease, neighbor, subsidy, and employment conflicts; dedicated agricultural variants exist — check availability.
- **Fixed-cost cover** — running costs after a major loss.

**Situational**
- **Product liability / closure cover for direct marketing** — the farm shop turns the farmer into a food business; verify the coverage cut.
- **Cyberversicherung** — milking, feeding, and barn control are IT systems today.
- **Gruppen-Unfall / bKV** — the agricultural statutory cover handles the work accident; contributing family members and leisure gaps are the conversation. Crop-yield and livestock-mortality covers are specialty lines with their own market — say so openly and place them separately.

# Part 2 — Spotting the need

German owner-managers rarely volunteer problems, distrust anything that smells like a pitch, and respect demonstrated industry knowledge above all. Discovery is matter-of-fact, document-anchored, and occasion-driven. Five mechanics carry the discipline, then the industry-by-industry cues.

**1. The Berufsgenossenschaft boundary as door-opener**
Every employer already pays statutory accident contributions, and most believe the cover reaches further than it does. Never argue against it — use it as the anchor: it covers employees' workplace and commuting accidents and occupational diseases; it does not cover leisure time (where most accidents happen), it pays no pain-and-suffering-level benefits, and the owner is — depending on the Berufsgenossenschaft — only voluntarily insured or not at all. Line to use: "The Berufsgenossenschaft covers your people's workplace accidents. The question is: what happens to you personally, and what happens Saturday at the football pitch?" The annual contribution notice is also the most natural document for verifying payroll and business description — which surfaces underinsurance and misclassified operations without it feeling like an interrogation.

**2. Occasion-driven advice beats cold pitching**
Business owners buy at life events of the business. The trigger list to monitor and ask about explicitly: founding or master-craftsman exam, relocation or extension, new machine or major investment, first hire, revenue jump, new webshop, PV on the roof, new vehicles, handover to the next generation. Each changes the risk profile and is simultaneously a legitimate, § 6 VVG-compliant reason to advise. Line to use: "What has changed in the business in the last two years — space, people, machines, revenue? The policy from back then knows nothing about any of it."

**3. Documents beat question lists**
An owner answers twenty questions grudgingly but will readily put the current policy, the latest endorsement, or the contribution notice on the table if you offer a concrete review. The underinsurance check — sum insured against today's replacement value, buffer, waiver — is the strongest first-meeting tool: it almost always produces a finding, it is factual, and it documents itself in the advice record.

**4. Listen for these lines — the universal tells**
"Nothing has ever happened here" (risk denial — enter via same-industry claim examples, never via fear). "My tax advisor / my wife / my broker handles that" (you've just learned the decision structure — involve the named third party, don't route around them). "We can't find people" (pivot to bKV, bAV, Gruppen-Unfall as employer positioning). "We have a webshop now / everything runs through the software" (Cyber and Elektronik). "The bank wants a guarantee" (Kautionsversicherung). "The warehouse is twice as full as it used to be" (underinsurance, immediately).

**5. The skilled-labor shortage is the biggest open door**
Employer-benefit lines (bKV, bAV, Gruppen-Unfall) are the one product family where the client brings the pain to you unprompted. Position them as an HR instrument, never as insurance: "This isn't an insurance topic, it's a retention argument for your people that costs less than one replacement hire."

## The cues by industry

### 1. Construction & Finishing Trades — *crews on other people's property, value in the van*
- **Post-handover liability:** "Who pays when your work causes damage months after acceptance?" — listen for callbacks, warranty visits, "we're in someone's house every day."
- **Tools in transit:** "What's the replacement value of what's sitting in the vans right now?" — listen for "my whole setup is in the bus," trailers loaded overnight.
- **Bauleistung / Montage:** "Are you on new builds, or installing high-value systems?" — listen for shell construction, delivered-but-uninstalled units on site.
- **Kaution:** "Are clients withholding retentions or demanding guarantees?" — listen for "the bank line is tight," public tenders.

### 2. Cleaning & Facility Services — *keys in hand, inside client space*
- **Key loss & working damage:** "Do you hold master keys or alarm codes?" — listen for after-hours access, "we have the master key."
- **Rechtsschutz (employment):** "How many people, and how much turnover in the team?" — listen for dismissal stories, wage disputes.
- **Fleet:** "How do the crews move between sites?" — listen for multiple objects serviced per day.
- **Cyber:** "Where do access codes and schedules live?" — listen for scheduling apps, client portals.

### 3. Landscaping & Grounds — *machines in the open, duties on other people's ground*
- **Winter service liability:** "Do you plow or grit in winter?" — listen for "snow keeps us busy year-round," seasonal contracts.
- **Mobile machinery:** "Does equipment stay on trailers or sites overnight?" — listen for "it all stays on the trailer."
- **Environmental modules:** "Do you apply plant-protection products?" — listen for "we treat lawns," spraying programs.
- **Bauleistung:** "Any larger new installations this season?" — listen for tender language, staged projects.

### 4. Food Service & Hospitality — *guests, food, and an authority that can close you*
- **Betriebsschließung:** "What happens if the health office closes you for a week?" — listen for inspection stories, hygiene audits.
- **Contents underinsurance:** "When did the sum insured last see your kitchen-equipment invoices?" — listen for recent renovation, new kitchen line.
- **Business interruption:** "How long can rent and payroll run on reserves?" — listen for thin-margin talk, high fixed rent.
- **bKV / benefits:** "How hard is hiring right now?" — listen for "we're closed Mondays because we have no staff."

### 5. Retail — *stock is the capital, footfall is the risk*
- **Seasonal underinsurance:** "What's on the floor and in the back at the seasonal peak — versus what's in the policy?" — listen for "the warehouse is full for Christmas."
- **Cyber / POS:** "What happens when the till system is down on a Saturday?" — listen for webshop mentions, card-only checkout.
- **Business interruption:** "Would the landlord pause the rent after a water damage? Neither would ours." — listen for prime-location rent talk.
- **Transport:** "Do goods move between branches or from your own pickups?" — listen for own van runs to the wholesaler.

### 6. Real Estate & Property Management — *other people's money and buildings*
- **Mandatory PI:** "Is your professional indemnity at the level your license requires?" — listen for new WEG mandates, growing units under management.
- **Elementar:** "Which buildings in the portfolio have natural-hazards cover?" — listen for heavy-rain stories, basement flooding.
- **Photovoltaik:** "Any PV going onto the roofs?" — listen for owner-association resolutions on solar.
- **Rechtsschutz:** "How many open tenancy disputes right now?" — listen for eviction cases, arrears.

### 7. Professional & Financial Services — *advice is the product, liability is personal*
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht:** "What happens if advice turns out demonstrably wrong and the client quantifies it?" — listen for growing mandate sizes, new licensed activities.
- **Cyber:** "Where does client data live, and who can reach it?" — listen for cloud tools, home-office setups.
- **Rechtsschutz:** "Ever had a fee go unpaid and thought about court?" — listen for receivables complaints.
- **Benefits:** "How do you keep good people from being poached?" — listen for hiring frustration.

### 8. IT & Digital Services — *your error becomes the client's downtime*
- **IT professional indemnity:** "If your update halts their production, who pays the standstill?" — listen for SLAs, go-live dates, managed-services contracts.
- **Own cyber cover:** "Would your clients stay if you were the one breached?" — listen for admin access to client systems.
- **Elektronik:** "What's the hardware worth in the office and the rack?" — listen for own server room, dev workstations.
- **Rechtsschutz:** "Any disputes over project acceptance?" — listen for "the client won't sign off."

### 9. Agencies, Marketing & Media — *published work, third-party rights*
- **Media liability / PI:** "Who clears image, music, and trademark rights — and who pays when it goes wrong?" — listen for stock-photo use, influencer campaigns.
- **Cyber:** "Do you hold client ad accounts and budgets?" — listen for "we run their whole performance spend."
- **Elektronik:** "What travels to a shoot in one van?" — listen for camera lists, rented gear.
- **Rechtsschutz:** "Ever fought over usage rights after a project ended?" — listen for scope-creep stories.

### 10. Healthcare & Care Services — *bodily contact plus the most sensitive data*
- **Professional liability:** "Who defends the treatment-error allegation — justified or not?" — listen for new treatment methods, delegated staff treatments.

### 12. Automotive Trades — *custody of customer vehicles*
- **Custody damage:** "Whose policy pays for the customer's car on your lot overnight — or on the test drive?" — listen for full yards, dealer plates.
- **Environmental:** "How are oils, fuels, and batteries handled and stored?" — listen for EV work, tank talk.
- **Contents / machinery:** "What would it cost to re-equip the workshop from zero?" — listen for new diagnostics, calibration rigs.
- **Rechtsschutz:** "How often do repair invoices get disputed?" — listen for warranty arguments.

### 13. Education & Training — *supervision duty, often over minors*
- **Supervision liability:** "Who is liable if a child is hurt during a lesson or on an excursion?" — listen for group sizes, off-site activities.
- **Cyber:** "Where do participant data and course platforms run?" — listen for online offerings, payment plans.
- **Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht:** "Do you certify outcomes or prepare people for examinations?" — listen for promised qualifications.

# Part 3 — Objection handling

**Five ground rules before the individual objections:**

1. **Price is the real objection less often than it sounds.** The dominant objection in German commercial is attachment: to the existing advisor, to running contracts with notice periods, to habit. Trust and relationship tenure beat premium.
2. **Hard closing destroys the conversation.** Pressure, artificial scarcity, and aggressive closing formulas activate a deep-seated distrust of insurance salespeople inherited from commission-driven structured sales. The close is the logical consequence of a clean, documented analysis — not a moment of overcoming resistance.
3. **Written follow-up is a friend, not an evasion.** "Send it to me in writing" is often a genuine process step, not a brush-off. Deliver it structured and fix a follow-up date, and you gain credibility from it. Without the fixed date, it was a brush-off after all — the date is the test.
4. **Notice periods structure the sales motion.** Annual contracts with three-month notice mean many conversations correctly end with a date in the calendar rather than a signature. A managed renewal-date and follow-up system is not the consolation prize — it *is* the pipeline.
5. **The advice record is a sales instrument.** The documented sentence "client was informed of coverage gap X and declined" focuses decision-makers like almost no other argument — never play it as a threat, present it as lived diligence.

**The objections, ranked by frequency:**

### 1. "My agent/broker handles all of that — I've been with him for 20 years." *(Incumbent / the most common objection)*
- **What's behind it:** Loyalty and convenience, not verified coverage quality. Twenty years of relationship often means twenty-year-old sums insured.
- **Reframe:** "That speaks well of you — good service is worth keeping, and I'm not here to displace anyone. The only question is: when was it last checked whether the contracts still match today's business? Companies change faster than policies."
- **Bridge:** "When did someone last hold your sums insured against today's stock value and payroll? That's exactly what I'm offering — no obligation, in writing, and if everything fits, I'll tell you exactly that."
- **Play:** Never attack the incumbent — it loses. Offer the factual second opinion with a documented result, and be transparent about your own role and mandate; that transparency pays straight into trust.

### 2. "I'm already insured through the Berufsgenossenschaft." *(Statutory/private confusion)*
- **What's behind it:** The most widespread coverage illusion in the market — a statutory employer obligation mistaken for comprehensive protection. Not resistance; a knowledge gap.
- **Reframe:** "Correct, and you need it. The Berufsgenossenschaft covers your employees' workplace accidents. It covers neither your liability toward customers, nor your equipment, nor you as the owner — those are three completely separate construction sites."
- **Bridge:** "Let's lay out cleanly what you already have by law and where genuinely nothing is in place — then we only talk about the real gaps."
- **Play:** Never pitch anything the client holds by statute. Handling that boundary cleanly — here and with motor liability — builds instant competence trust and separates you from every salesperson they've met before.

### 3. "Send me something in writing first." *(Stall / culture)*
- **What's behind it:** Partly a genuine review wish, partly a polite brush-off. The difference reveals itself in whether a date gets attached.
- **Reframe:** "Gladly — that's part of my process anyway; you'll get the analysis in writing and traceable. So the paper is actually worth something to you: which two or three points should definitely be in it?"
- **Bridge:** "I'll send it by Thursday. Does early next week work — fifteen minutes to walk through the two flagged points?"
- **Play:** Don't push back on the written form — deliver it, structure it, and fix the follow-up in the same breath. The document plus the kept deadline *is* the sales argument.

### 4. "My contract still runs until..." *(Notice period)*
- **What's behind it:** Usually simply true. Annual contracts with three-month notice are the norm.
- **Reframe:** "Completely fine — which makes now exactly the right time: we review calmly, without pressure, and if it fits better, we pre-book the renewal date. And some things have no incumbent at all: a cyber policy or a group accident cover you can arrange today, independent of it."
- **Bridge:** "When is your main renewal date? I'll set our review three months before it — and until then we close the gaps that are already open today."
- **Play:** The answer is a system, not rhetoric: renewal-date list, follow-up discipline, cancellation support — plus consistently selling the incumbent-free gaps (cyber, legal expenses, benefits) ahead of the renewal date.

### 5. "Let me think about it." *(Stall)*
- **What's behind it:** Usually one unspoken concern rather than a real need for time — though sometimes genuine deliberation. The two are distinguishable: real deliberation accepts a concrete follow-up date; the brush-off avoids one.
- **Reframe:** "Of course — it's your business and it deserves consideration. In my experience, when someone wants to think it over, one specific point isn't settled yet. Which one is it for you — the cover, the cost, or the timing?"
- **Bridge:** "What information would make the decision easy for you? I'll bring exactly that to the follow-up — does Tuesday work?"
- **Play:** Respect genuine deliberation instead of trying to break it. The winning combination is the same as for the written-form objection: a structured summary plus a fixed date. A scheduled second conversation converts better than a pushed first one.

### 6. "Too expensive." / "I saw it cheaper elsewhere." *(Price)*
- **What's behind it:** Premium viewed in isolation, not against the risk and almost never against the quality of the conditions. Comparison-portal prices rarely compare equal cover.
- **Reframe:** "I understand — more expensive than what, exactly? Than another quote, or than what you'd budgeted? And on the other quote: same waiver of underinsurance, same cover for damage to the property being worked on? That's exactly where the differences sit — the ones you only see at claim time."
- **Bridge:** "What would a single uninsured loss from your own trade cost you — the water damage after handover, the master key? That's the number the premium competes against, not the portal price."
- **Play:** Never answer with a discount — it damages your advisor position. The conditions argument wins: replacement value, buffer, waiver of underinsurance, and — where the tariff includes one — a conditions-update guarantee that keeps the contract from aging. Name the feature, confirm the detail in the current conditions.

### 7. "I need to ask my tax advisor first." *(Authority)*
- **What's behind it:** The Steuerberater is the most trusted external advisor in the Mittelstand. Not an excuse — the real decision structure, and an opportunity.
- **Reframe:** "Good idea, he knows your numbers. So he can assess it quickly, I'll summarize the recommendation with reasoning on one page — deductibility, payroll linkage, all on it. On bAV and bKV he'll want a say anyway."
- **Bridge:** "Shall we set the follow-up so you have his okay by then — or bring him in directly on the benefits topics?"
- **Play:** Never route around the tax advisor — equip and involve him. On bAV and bKV he is a de-facto co-decider, and a convinced Steuerberater refers you onward. Leave tax statements cleanly to him: no tax advice from the intermediary.

### 8. "Doesn't my Betriebshaftpflicht already cover all of that?" *(Coverage assumption)*
- **What's behind it:** The liability policy mistaken for all-round protection. The standard confusions: own stock and equipment (needs contents cover), own downtime (business interruption), pure financial loss (Vermögensschaden-Haftpflicht), cyber, and the legal costs of the firm's own disputes (Rechtsschutz).
- **Reframe:** "The Betriebshaftpflicht pays when you damage *others*. Everything that happens *to you* — your stock, your downtime, your data, your own legal disputes — is a different policy. It's the foundation, not the roof."
- **Bridge:** "Shall we sort your risks into two buckets — damage you cause versus damage you suffer? Then you see in one view what the liability policy carries and what currently nothing carries."
- **Play:** The two-bucket explanation (liability outward vs. own losses inward) is the cleanest framing there is, and it works across every industry in Part 1. Draw it on paper — it survives the meeting.

### 9. "Nothing has happened here in 30 years." *(Risk denial)*
- **What's behind it:** Lived experience beats statistics — understandable and internally consistent.
- **Reframe:** "And that speaks for your business. Only: you don't insure the past, you insure the one year that goes differently. The colleague with the water damage also had 25 good years before it."
- **Bridge:** "Which loss would genuinely hit your business — not the scratch, but the one that takes six months to recover from? That's the only one we'll talk about; the rest we leave out."
- **Play:** Fear scenarios and drama repel. What works is sober, same-industry claim examples — ideally regional — and the visible willingness to leave out what's unnecessary.

### 10. "I don't want to be over-insured — you people always just want to sell." *(Trust)*
- **What's behind it:** The legacy of commission-driven structured sales. The client is protecting himself from the salesperson, not from the insurance.
- **Reframe:** "I understand the objection, and it's legitimate — which is why I document every recommendation with reasoning in the advice record, and I document just as clearly what you *don't* need. If I name two things today you can save yourself, will you take seriously the one gap I genuinely mean?"
- **Bridge:** "Let's start with what can go or is duplicated — after that we talk about what's missing."
- **Play:** The statutory advice and documentation duty is the strongest anti-distrust tool you have. Deselect first, recommend second — the order is the message.

### 11. "Insurers never pay when it matters anyway." *(Trust)*
- **What's behind it:** An experienced or retold claims disappointment — almost always caused by underinsurance, breached policyholder duties (Obliegenheiten), or a never-reviewed policy.
- **Reframe:** "Those stories exist, and almost always the same thing is behind them: the sum was too low, or a duty was breached, and nobody knew either beforehand. That's exactly why we check it *before* the claim — the waiver of underinsurance and the duties you need to keep are in my record, not in the fine print."
- **Bridge:** "May I show you, on your current policy, the two or three places where it typically jams at claim time?"
- **Play:** Underinsurance with proportional reduction (Quotelung) is the most common real-world reason for cut payouts — which makes the underinsurance check the direct answer to this objection. Explain Obliegenheiten and the § 19 VVG disclosure duty proactively; transparency about the rules of the game is the sales argument.

### 12. "Money's tight right now." *(Price / cash flow)*
- **What's behind it:** Often a real liquidity constraint, not a negotiating move. Treat it as a prioritization signal.
- **Reframe:** "Then we should be doubly careful that a single loss doesn't hit exactly that tight liquidity. Let's do triage: which risk would break the business, and which would merely annoy it? We cover the first category and consciously park the rest — documented."
- **Bridge:** "If we close only the one existential gap today and put the rest on the renewal review — which gap would that be for you?"
- **Play:** No discounts. Check payment-cadence options in the current tariff rather than promising them. Record the conscious deferral in the advice record: the documented "reviewed and postponed" protects the client, protects you under § 61 VVG, and creates the legitimate re-entry point.

### 13. "My business is too small for all that." *(Cross-sell)*
- **What's behind it:** Covers like cyber, legal expenses, or business interruption perceived as corporate topics.
- **Reframe:** "The other way round: the large company absorbs the loss from petty cash — for you, the whole business hangs on the same loss. That's exactly why compact SME tariffs exist: cut for your size."
- **Bridge:** "How many weeks of standstill can your account absorb before it gets serious? That's the only number that decides 'too small'."
- **Play:** Point to the compact SME product lines as visible proof the cover was built for this size of business — then keep the recommendation to the one or two covers that map to an existential loss, not a catalogue.

### 14. "Cyber doesn't concern us — we're not an IT company." *(Cross-sell / the most common product-specific objection)*
- **What's behind it:** Cyber read as a technology topic, not as a business-interruption and payment-fraud topic.
- **Reframe:** "Attackers aren't looking for IT companies, they're looking for open doors — and the invoice with the altered bank details hits the trade business exactly like the law firm. The question isn't whether you sell IT; it's whether your business runs for a week without email, till, and bookkeeping."
- **Bridge:** "What concretely happens at your firm if no computer opens tomorrow morning and the last outgoing invoice was paid to the wrong account?"
- **Play:** The effective entry runs through business interruption and payment-diversion fraud, not through data-protection theory; GDPR notification duties are the factual supporting argument, not the lead.

### 15. "I'll add the rest later." *(Cross-sell deferral)*
- **What's behind it:** Agreement in principle, deferral in practice — and "later" never arrives without a trigger. The notice-period rhythm makes "later" feel especially legitimate.
- **Reframe:** "Fine — but between now and later, the gap is real. Let's do two things: sort what genuinely can wait from what can't, and put 'later' in the calendar. An open-ended 'later' is a friendly no, and neither of us needs that."
- **Bridge:** "Which date do we write down for the second tranche — and is there anything on the list that would keep you up at night before then?"
- **Play:** Anchor "later" to the concrete renewal date and enter the deferral in the advice record. A dated, documented commitment gets kept in a way a verbal "we'll circle back" never is.

### 16. "Now's not a good time — come back after the season." *(Stall)*
- **What's behind it:** For seasonal businesses (hospitality, landscaping, events, retail) often simply true — and simultaneously risky, because peak season is peak exposure.
- **Reframe:** "Precisely because this is your strongest period, it's also your biggest risk — full warehouse, full order book, full floor. I don't need a decision on everything today: just the one gap that hurts during the season, and a firm date afterwards for the rest."
- **Bridge:** "Which week after season-end shall I book firmly — and until then, may I at least have the sum insured adjusted for the full warehouse?"
- **Play:** Resolve it through a firmly agreed, documented follow-up date rather than forcing a first-contact close. A dated "no, but in November" is, as a rule, kept — treat it as pipeline, not as a loss.

---

**Bottom line on objection work:** Four objections set the direction of most conversations — incumbent attachment, the statutory-cover confusion, the written-form preference, and contract notice periods. Master those four, run a renewal-date system, and use the advice record visibly as proof of diligence, and you need no closing technique. The rest is conditions quality: replacement value, premium-free buffer, waiver of underinsurance — name the feature, confirm the number in the current conditions.
